## Context

What forces are in play? What problem exists, what constraints bound the answer? Write it so a developer joining in two years understands why this was ever a question.

## Decision

The decision, in active voice, in as few sentences as survive precision. If the decision has parts, number them.

## Costs & sharp edges (be honest)

The section that makes an ADR worth reading. Every real decision has costs — name them before your critics do. What gets harder? What new failure modes appear? What discipline must the team now maintain, and what happens when they don't? An ADR with an empty costs section is marketing.

## Alternatives considered

For each serious alternative: what it was, why it lost. One paragraph each. "We didn't think of anything else" is an honest and occasionally correct entry.

## Consequences

**Positive** — what improves.
**Negative** — what regresses (should echo the costs section).
**Neutral** — what merely changes.
